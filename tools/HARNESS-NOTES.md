# Harness notes: the render commands

Decisions taken by the harness builder while building `film`, `seq`, `sheet`, `loop`, `render:local` and `stress`. Facts marked MEASURED were run in the builder's VM (4 cores, software WebGL).

## film

- Defaults: `--which b`, `--format preview`, `--tier draft`, `--assets full`, the rate `film.previewFps`.
- A page is rendered in pieces of at most 300 rendered frames, split evenly, never more than `sandbox.maxFramesOnDisk`. The split depends only on the page, the rate and the limit, so every VM cuts a page at the same frames. `--piece N` changes the limit.
- Frames wait as PNG in `out/work/film/frames/<piece>/` with a note of the build they belong to. A piece is encoded as soon as its last frame is written, checked with `ffprobe` for its frame count, and its frames are deleted. Only one piece's frames are ever on disk: the folder of any other piece is deleted before a piece starts.
- A stopped run carries on at the frame where it stopped: frames on disk whose note matches the build are kept, a piece with a matching record is not rendered again, a page's chunk with a matching record is not joined again.
- Pieces land in `out/work/film/chunks/pieces/`, a page's chunk in `out/work/film/chunks/<page>-F-T.mp4`, each with a record beside it. A chunk is its pieces joined with `ffmpeg -c copy`.
- Encoding: `libx264`, high profile, the bitrate of the format as the target with 1.5 times as the ceiling, a key picture every second, closed groups (`-flags +cgop`), no scene cut pictures, bt709 tags, time base 90000. Every piece starts on a key picture, so pieces and chunks join with no picture encoded twice.
- A record matches when everything but `machine` is the same: hashes, format, tier, asset set, diagnostic, rate, codec, bitrate, range, and the browser version when a browser is open. This is what lets a chunk rendered in another VM be joined here. With `--join` no browser is opened, so the browser version is not compared either.
- `--first N --last M` renders exactly the frames of that range that fall on the preview rate and writes a clip `out/work/film-<which>-F-T-<first>-<last>.mp4` named by the frames it really holds. It never writes the whole film's file.
- `--unit ID` renders that page's chunk and joins nothing.
- `--join` joins the page chunks of this build that exist, in film order. If pages are missing it says `NOT THE WHOLE FILM`, lists them, and the record says `complete: false`.
- Sound: when `audio/mix/review.wav` exists it is laid under the joined film as `export.audioCodec` at `export.audioKbps`. A film with pages missing, or a clip, gets the stretches of the mix that belong to its pictures. Film A gets silence under its opening.
- `--which a` renders the opening's chunk (`opening-F-T.mp4`) and joins it in front of film B's chunks, which are shared and never rendered for it again. Should the page say the opening is not built, the command says so and exits 1 before it renders anything.
- `--from-hd` scales `film-<which>-hd-T.mp4` down to `formats.preview` at `previewFilm.bitrateMbps` and copies its sound. It refuses a film whose record is not of this build.
- `--review` renders film B and writes it as `out/work/review-cut.mp4` (`export.files.reviewCut`).
- `--chunks-to DIR` copies finished chunks with their records, and under `DIR/pieces/` the pieces of pages that are not whole. `--chunks-from DIR` takes both in, after checking each file against the hash in its record.
- `--minutes M` stops cleanly after M minutes, prints `NOT FINISHED`, and exits 2. Exit 1 is kept for "cannot do its work".
- `--by NAME` names who made the looks. A look is written every `status.minGapMinutes` of rendering.
- Free disk is checked first. Under `sandbox.minFreeDiskGb` it deletes, in this order and stopping as soon as there is room: waiting frames, `seq`, `determinism`, `roughcut`, `stress`, `crops`, `stills`.

## sheet

- One sheet is at most `critics.contactSheetFrames` pictures and at most `critics.maxImageLongEdgePx` on its long edge. With 12 pictures of 16 by 9 that is 3 columns by 4 rows with tiles 461 px wide. A heading sits above the grid, a label under each picture: time in the film, beat, frame. Nothing is drawn over a picture.
- Files: `out/work/sheets/sheet-<kind>-<id>-[NofM]-F-T.jpg`, each with a record beside it and one line in the look log. A sheet whose record matches this build is not rendered again (`--force` renders it anyway).
- `--beat ID`: 12 frames spread evenly over the beat. `--unit ID`: one sheet for every 12 s of the page, the frames spread evenly (`--sheets N` sets the number). `--film`: one sheet for each page, 12 frames each (`--sheets N` spreads N sheets over the whole film instead). `--pages`: the six pages at rest through `renderRest`.
- `--loop` and `--opening`: 12 frames spread over the loop or the opening. Should the page say they are not built, the command says so and exits 1.
- `--motion NAME`: the frame comes from the events of `filmState`, read in the browser without rendering. `hit` is the first event with `hitsCursor`, or whose name ends in `hit-cursor`; `page-turn` and `bumper` are the events of those names; `reassembly` is `strips-zip`; any other name is taken as an event name. `--nth K` takes the K-th, `--unit ID` looks in one page only. The sheet is 12 frames in a row at the preview rate with a third of them before the event; `--every-frame` uses every film frame.
- `--live`: the live site's own mode with a bot as the driver (`--bot`, default the first of `live.bots`; `--seed`, default the `seed` of the config) and a clock that is stepped, not real time. 12 moments spread over the run's length, which is read from `out/work/bots.json` or given with `--seconds`. Labels are the run's time, the page and the last gag reached. It uses `src/main.ts` as the Builder wrote it: `&bot=NAME&seed=N&clock=steps` in live mode and `window.liveRun = { state(), advance(seconds) }`. The patch I asked for is gone from the branch: main has the same calls.
- The stub pages emit `bolt-hit-cursor` with `hitsCursor` false, so `hit` also goes by the name.

## seq

- `npm run seq -- --first N --last M` writes every film frame from N to M, at `film.fps`, as `out/work/seq/frame-NNNNNN-F-T.png`, each with a note of its build beside it. `--step K` writes every K-th frame. `--format` and `--tier` default to `preview` and `draft`; `--diag NAME` renders a diagnostic view.
- A run is at most `sandbox.maxFramesOnDisk` frames. A longer range is refused with a sentence that says so.
- A frame of this build that is already there is kept (`--force` renders it again). The run's record is `out/work/seq/seq-<first>-<last>-F-T.json`, and its look line is the middle frame.
- `--opening` and `--loop` write frames of the opening and of the card loop (`opening-frame-...`, `loop-frame-...`). Should the page say those are not built, the command says so and exits 1.

## render:local

- Everything it writes is under `out/work/render-local/`. Rate `film.fps`, `export.local.videoCodec` in `export.local.container`. It uses the same pieces, records and resume as `film`: a page is rendered in pieces (120 frames for a master, so about 2 s of frames wait on disk at a time), a stopped run carries on at the frame where it stopped, pieces are joined with `ffmpeg -c copy`.
- `--proof`: the harness's headless browser with software graphics, `formats.preview`, `draft`, `export.proofSeconds` of film B in a row across the first hit on the cursor in step 1 (a third of the frames before the hit), and the first `export.proofSeconds` of the opening. Files `out/work/render-local/proof/proof-film-b.mov` and `proof-opening.mov`; the record `out/work/render-local/proof.json` holds `engineHash`, `pageHashes` and `parts: [{ what, file }]` with the `ffprobe` result of each part. Each file is checked for its frame count, size and rate.
- `--proof` alone makes both parts. Should the page say the opening is not built, it exits 1 after writing film B's part. `--proof --what film-b` makes film B's part only and exits 0; `--proof --what film-a` makes the opening's part only.
- `--what film-b|film-a|loop` with `--test` or `--go` uses the installed Chrome in a window with its graphics card on. It is looked for at the usual place of the system, or at `WJA_CHROME`. No browser is downloaded: with no Chrome it exits 1 and says where it looked.
- No master with software graphics: when the browser reports a software renderer and the format is `formats.master`, it exits 1 unless `deliver.sandboxMaster` is true. `--software --format preview --tier draft` runs the `--test` and `--go` paths in this workspace, which is how they were tested here.
- `--test`: one judged still (the first of `stills` that has a kept copy in `out/stills/`) is rendered in the kept still's format and tier, written beside it as `out/work/render-local/test/<name>-beside.jpg` with the PSNR, both sides as JPEG of the same settings so that two identical renders give `identical`. Under `quality.crossMachineMinPsnr` it says so in the last line. Then `npm run take -- --check`, then the first `export.local.testSeconds` seconds as `<name>-test.mov` with the mix, opened with the system's own command (`--no-open` leaves it shut). `--skip-still` and `--skip-take` leave a step out.
- NOT DONE: the search for a take on this machine when `take --check` fails. `tools/take.mjs` is not built, so there is no way yet to search into a file other than the committed one, or to tell the site to use it. Today a failing check is printed, named in the last line, and the command exits 1 after the test render.
- `--go`: checks first that the film reports its length times `film.fps` frames, renders every page, joins, and checks that the file holds that many frames, at the right size and rate. Names: `export.files.filmB`, `filmA`, and the stem of `cardLoop`, with `.mov`.
- `--what film-a`: the opening as its own chunk in front of film B's chunks, which are reused when they are of this build.
- `--what loop`: every frame of the loop at `film.fps`, and `card-still.jpg` from its frame 0.
- Sound: the mix is looked for in `audio/final/` as `film-b.*`, then `<export.files.filmB>.*`, `final-b.*`, `b.*`, then any sound file with `b` as a word of its name (the same with `a`). The kit does not name the files: UNKNOWN until the mix exists. In the `.mov` it is laid in as 24 bit PCM at 48 kHz, since the kit names no sound codec for the master. `--mix FILE` names the mix outright. With no mix the file is silent and the last line says so.
- UNKNOWN: the installed Chrome path was never run. This workspace has no Chrome and no screen.

## loop

- Defaults `--format` `deliver.cardLoop.format`, `--tier draft`. The loop's frames are indexed at `film.fps`; every `film.fps / deliver.cardLoop.fps`-th is rendered. It refuses a loop whose `frameCount` is not `deliver.cardLoop.seconds` times `film.fps`.
- Pieces and resume as in `film`, under `out/work/loop/chunks/`. `--first N --last M` renders a range and joins nothing; `--join` joins the ranges that exist and opens a browser only if the picture of frame 0 is missing.
- Files: `out/work/loop/card-loop.mp4` (the chunk, `export.videoCodec` at `export.bitrateMbps`), `card-loop-web.mp4` (`webWidth` wide, even height, `webBitrateMbps`, encoded once from the loop), `card-still.jpg` (frame 0, rendered as a picture of its own, never taken from the film file), each with a record.
- The seam: with a browser open, the frame one past the last is rendered and compared with frame 0 pixel for pixel. The result is in the record; when they differ the command exits 1.
- The opening's tier: `renderOpeningFrame(i, tier)` takes it, as the film and the loop do.
- The seam. `renderLoopFrame` wraps its index, so the frame one past the last is frame 0 by construction and that check only proves the wrap. What a viewer sees is the step from the last picture to the first, so `loop` also writes `loop-seam-F-T.jpg` (not named `card-`, so that `keep --deliver` leaves it): the last three pictures and the first three, read back from the written loop, each labelled with how much it differs from the one before it in dB. When the step over the seam is larger than every step beside it the command says so. It does not exit 1 for that: the config names no limit for it.

## stress

- The four moments come from the keys of the config: `step2-rest` (a page at rest), `step1-fire`, `reply-all` (the office tier in "Reply All"), `boss-fight`. The frame is the key's place in its beat, and the half second is that many frames in a row with a third of them before it.
- Defaults `--format hd`, `--tier draft`, rate `film.previewFps` (15 frames): the rate at which the films of this workspace are encoded. `--fps 60` uses every frame. `--moment ID` does one moment.
- Each moment is encoded with the film's own encoder settings at `export.bitrateMbps` and at `quality.compressionStressMbps`. Files under `out/work/stress/`: the two films, a still from each (the frame of the key, decoded), the frame as rendered, and a sheet `stress-<id>-F-T.jpg` of the three whole pictures over the three in their own pixels round the cursor, each labelled under it with the bitrate asked for, the bitrate got and the PSNR against the rendered frame. A record sits beside the sheet, and a look line is added.
- Half a second is short for an encoder that aims at an average, so each encode is made in two passes. The bitrate got is printed beside the one asked for. It is above the target, because half a second holds the key picture of its second and so more than half of that second's bits (MEASURED on the merged pages, `node tools/stress.mjs`: 22.7 to 26.6 Mbps for 20 asked, 8.2 to 9.7 for 5 asked, PSNR 46.2 to 47.7 dB at 20 and 45.9 to 47.0 dB at 5, four moments in 1.6 min). The still is the frame of the key, a third of the way in, which is not the key picture.
- The still is decoded with exact rounding (`accurate_rnd+full_chroma_int`). Without it a flat ground of 204,201,199 came back as 202,200,198 and the PSNR read 41 dB for a picture that was right.

## keep --film

- Keeps the newest whole preview of this build: `out/work/film-b-preview-<tier>.mp4` as `out/<export.files.previewB>` and, when there is one, film A as `out/<export.files.previewA>`, each with a `.json` record beside it (build hashes, frames, seconds, bitrate, sha256, the file it came from).
- Exit 1 with the reasons when there is no whole film B of this build. Exit 2 when a file is at or over `repo.maxFileMb`. Nothing under `out/` is committed by this command.

## parts

- A file carries a part when the part's name is written in it as a flag. The source is read as a syntax tree (`parseAst` of vite, which is installed; the `typescript` package here has no compiler API in JS). A string is a flag when it is the value of `part:` or `standIn:`, the right side of `.part =` or `.userData.standIn =`, the default of a parameter named so, or reaches one of those through a constant, `??`, `||`, `?:`, an array or object of a constant, or a parameter and the calls that fill it, in the file or through its imports. A name in a type, a phase or a label is not a flag. JSON under `src/`, `public/` and `assets-src/` is read for `"part"` and `"standIn"`.
- A name made at run time (a template) is kept as a template and gives its file to every part of the register the template can make. None exists today.
- `standInHash` is sha256 over, for each file in sorted order: its name, a zero byte, its bytes, a zero byte. `commit` is the whole hash of `HEAD`. A recorded hash is never written again; only a part that is `stand-in` with an empty hash is filled.
- `--check` exits 2 when a flag in the code or reported by the page is not in the register, or the page reports a part that no file carries; 0 otherwise. It writes `out/work/parts-check.json`. The page is asked through `filmState` once a second along the film (`--every S` changes that) and `info().standIns` after one picture of each page, the opening and the loop. `--no-page` opens no browser.
- `--set NAME --state S` takes any of the five states and records `HEAD`. It does not touch `files` or `standInHash`.
- Parts that no file carries keep empty `files`, `standInHash` and `commit`: there is nothing to hash, and an invented list of files would make G13 compare the wrong thing.

## simcache

- Made in Node from the stage exactly as `src/app.ts` makes it for a page of the film: `new Stage({ mode: 'film', flow: filmFlow(unit), pages, driver: new ScriptDriver(), film: filmOptions(unit) })`, one stage a page of `film.units`. A checkpoint is the text of the stage's `save()`.
- One checkpoint at step 0 of every page of `film.units`, one every `sim.checkpointSeconds`, and one on the step at which the stage enters a later page of the same unit (the careers page at the end of `decision`). Files `out/work/simcache/<unit>-<frame>-step-<step>.json`; `index.json` holds `engineHash`, `sharedHash`, `pageHashes`, `stepHz`, `fps`, the first frame of every unit, and for each checkpoint its unit, page, step, frame, file, sha256 and bytes.
- `--check` reads the files (a file whose sha256 is not the index's is a problem), starts a fresh stage at each checkpoint, checks that the state loaded is the state the file holds, runs to the step of the next and compares the text of `save()` with the next file. Exit 2 when a replay differs, a file is wrong, or the checkpoints are of another build; exit 1 when there is no index.
- The render commands hand the page the checkpoint nearest before the first frame they draw of a piece (`handCheckpoint` in `tools/lib/film-render.mjs`), when the index is of this build and the page has `window.showcase.useCheckpoint`. A checkpoint that falls inside a frame, after its first step, is not used for that frame.
- The page's side is `tools/HARNESS-NEEDS.patch`: `useCheckpoint(unit, saved)` puts the save into the stage of that page with `Stage.restore`, and `Stage.goto` already starts from the latest checkpoint before the step asked for, so `renderFilmFrame` and `filmState` need no other change. The kit's interface in 05 does not list `useCheckpoint`.
- Events before the checkpoint are not in the page after a start from one: `filmState` of a frame before the checkpoint's own gives no events in that session. The harness only hands a checkpoint at or before the first frame it draws.

## hostcheck

- The clean copy is `git archive HEAD` unpacked into `out/work/hostcheck/clean/`, without the top folders that `.vercelignore` lists: what the host gets. `--whole` copies every tracked file (a plain clean clone). Uncommitted changes are never in it; the command says how many there are. The copy is deleted at the end unless `--keep`.
- In the copy: `npm ci --no-audit --no-fund`, then `npm run build`. Their output is in `out/work/hostcheck/install.log` and `build.log`. The build time held against `host.maxBuildMinutes` is the install plus the build, because the host does both. On a machine with a warm package cache the install is far shorter than on a cold host.
- Sizes: every file of the copy's `dist/`. A megabyte is 1048576 bytes.
- First load: the copy's `dist/` is served alone (no `assets-full/` beside it), opened at `/` with no query in the headless browser at 1920 by 1080. Every request made until `window.showcase.ready` has resolved and two frames have been drawn is counted once, with the bytes of the file as it is stored in `dist/`. The same sum with text files gzipped is given beside it as `compressedMb`; the limit is held against the stored bytes, the larger number. A screenshot goes to `out/work/hostcheck/first-picture.png`.
- "Opens the careers page in live mode" is true when the page got ready with no `mode` in the address, reports the flag `careers-page`, and had no error, no failed request and no request for a file that is not in `dist/`.
- Exit 0 inside every limit; 2 outside one, or when the first load could not be measured because the built site did not get ready; 1 when the copy, the install or the build fails. `out/work/hostcheck.json` is written in every case and holds `verdict.rows` (name, got, limit, inside), `inside`, `opensCareersLive`, `firstLoad.files`, `dist.largest`, `install`, `build`, `copy`, `commit`.

## gate: G13, stand-ins

Written by the gate builder in Phase 3. Facts marked MEASURED were run in the builder's VM (software WebGL) at the commit named in `pipeline/phase3/gate.md`.

- Runs from Phase 3. `npm run gate -- --phase 3 --only G13 --unit step1` covers a unit before it is marked: `--unit` takes one unit or several with commas, any of the six pages, `site` and `film`.
- What it covers is `coveredUnits` in `tools/lib/gate-checks.mjs`, as `kit/07-QUALITY-BAR.md` says: the units named with `--unit`; every unit whose row under "Units" in `kit/PROGRESS.md` says `finished`; in Phase 5 and later, once the row of `site` says `in progress` or `finished` or holds a round, every unit but `film`; in Phase 6, once the row of `film` does, every unit. A part belongs to the unit the register gives it. When nothing is covered the check passes and says that it covers no part.
- Test 1 reads `state` in `parts/PARTS.json`. `stand-in` fails, and so does a word that is not a state.
- Test 2 fails when the part has no recorded files or no recorded hash; when one of its recorded files is gone today (a file that was moved would otherwise pass as well as one that was finished); when the hash of its files today is the recorded hash; when the record names its commits (`hashAt`) and the recorded hash is not the hash of those files at those commits, read again with `git show`, so a hash that was typed in or copied cannot pass; and, for a record that names no commit, always: such a record was not made by `--record`, and only a record taken from the stand-in commits can show a change that happened after them. The last rule exists because of what was MEASURED: with every first version merged (c534a17), `type`, `keyboard`, `turrets`, `slabs`, `paper` and `shredder` passed test 2 on their old records only because `src/pages/step1/layout.ts` and `src/pages/step1/index.ts`, which those records name, had changed for other reasons. And 46 of the 53 hashes in the register were recorded early in Phase 1 or 2 and their files changed again before the end of Phase 2, so "differs from the recorded hash" was already true for them while every one of them was a stand-in.
- Test 3 has three readings. The page's word along the film: `filmState(i).standIns` for every frame of the unit at `film.previewFps`, in one browser session, with the number of frames and the first frame and the beats in which a part is reported. The pixels: the `standins` view of every key, sheet and judged still of the unit, at hd, drawn with one sample a pixel (the live path at the first level of `live.quality`), so that no pixel is a mix of two meshes; a pixel counts for a part when it is within 2 codes of the part's flag colour (`standInColour` of `src/diagnostics/views.ts`, written as the sRGB code of the linear value). Pixels of another part's colour do not count against a part. The page's word after the pictures: `info().standIns` of that session. For the unit `film` the pictures are the first and last frame of the opening and the first of the loop.
- `npm run gate -- --phase 3 --only G13 --unit step1 --register FILE` runs the check on a copy of the register, to try the records and states before they are written to `parts/PARTS.json`. The note of the check says that it was a trial.
- The pixels along the film: `filmMoments` gives one moment a second of the unit's film time, its last frame, and the frame in which the page first reports each part. Each is drawn as a still of that beat and place in the beat through the camera `film`, with one sample a pixel, in the `standins` view, and read the same way. A still is never drawn in the middle of a page turn (`App.momentOf` moves it), so the frames of a turn are read by the page's word alone. MEASURED on step 1: 40 moments, 47 pictures with the keys and sheets, 22.7 s for the check. `slabs` is in no key and no sheet and shows 47001 flagged pixels in film frame 1916.
- MEASURED on step 1 today: one sample a pixel gives 5 colours in the `standins` view of `step1-fire` and the draft tier gives 12, the extra 7 being mixes on edges. A reading by nearness to a mix named `light-engine`, `page-turn` and `cursor-effects` as flagged with 1 to 956 pixels although no mesh carries them, which is why the pictures are drawn with one sample and read exactly.
- What test 3 cannot see: a part that no code path flags. Today these are `materials`, `light-engine`, `page-turn`, `cursor-effects` and `acting-step1`. For them tests 1 and 2 are the whole check, which is why test 2 must not pass by accident.
- It writes `out/work/gate/G13.json`: the units covered and why, the frames read, the pictures, and for each part its state, files and every reason it failed. The check takes about 8 s for step 1 (MEASURED).
- It builds `dist/` first when the source has changed (`ensureBuild`), before it loads the source in Node for the flag colours.

## parts: --record

- `npm run parts -- --record NAME --files a,b,c` records the files of a part and their hash as they were while the part was a stand-in. It is for the parts that carry no flag in a file, which `npm run parts` cannot fill, and with `--replace` for a part whose record is old or names a file that is gone.
- Each file is hashed as it was at the latest of the stand-in commits that holds it. The stand-in commits are `ANCHORS` in `tools/lib/gate-checks.mjs`: `729ee56` (the seam as handed to the builders of Phase 3, every maker null), `4b65361` (the first commit of Phase 3), `d0f83c8` (the last commit of Phase 2). `--at COMMIT[,COMMIT]` names others. The bytes come from `git show COMMIT:file`, never from the working tree, so the record is the same whenever it is made, also after the part is finished.
- The record holds `files` (sorted), `standInHash` (the same sha256 as the other parts: name, zero byte, bytes, zero byte for each file), `hashAt` (the whole hash of the commit each file was read at) and `commit` (`HEAD`). `--set` keeps `hashAt`.
- It refuses, exit 1, and writes nothing: a part that is not in the register; no files; a file that is not in the tree today; a part that has a hash already, unless `--replace` is given; a clone that does not hold the commits.
- A file that none of the stand-in commits holds is recorded as `absent` in `hashAt`, with no bytes, and the tool says so in a line of its own. It was not there while the part was a stand-in, so test 2 fails while the file is missing or empty and passes as soon as it holds a byte. That is weaker than a file that held the stand-in: name such a file only for a part that is made in a new file, as `page-turn` in `src/stage/turn.ts`. For a maker name the stub that exported null.
- Why the latest commit first and not `d0f83c8` alone, MEASURED with `git diff --stat d0f83c8 729ee56 -- src`: 29 files of `src/` changed between the two while every part stayed a stand-in, among them `src/shell/materials.ts`, `src/core/renderer.ts` and `src/stage/stage.ts`. Against `d0f83c8` those files would differ before anybody had made anything.
- What the rule cannot check: that the files named are the files the part is really made in. G13 prints the files of every part that fails test 2 and writes them all to `out/work/gate/G13.json`, so a reader can see what was compared.
- `--register FILE` works on another register than `parts/PARTS.json`. The tests use it.

## counts: by part

- `npm run counts -- --key ID | --id ID | --page P --beat B --at F [--live LEVEL] [--format hd] [--assets web]` prints, for one picture, what the renderer draws, a row for each part, sorted by draw calls. It needs `dist/` (`npm run build`).
- A row is the part named in `userData.part` or `userData.standIn` of the drawn object. Three rows of their own: `type` (every object with `userData.type`), `cursor` (every part whose name starts with `cursor`) and `ground` (an object of `shell` that carries no `userData.shape`). The part names inside such a row are printed in brackets. `made` says real, stand-in or both.
- The columns: meshes drawn (the objects drawn in any pass, each once: a mesh that is out of the picture and still casts a shadow into it is one), draw calls in the colour pass, in the extra pass the renderer makes for materials that let light through (shown only when used), in each shadow pass (named by the light whose shadow camera it is; a point light is six faces in one column), all calls, triangles.
- How it counts: `countParts(spec)` on `window.showcase` draws the still once to settle, then draws it again with `onBeforeRender` and `onBeforeShadow` of the `Object3D` prototype replaced, and those of every object that has its own wrapped. The renderer calls the first once for every draw of the colour pass and the second once for every draw of a shadow pass, after its own tests of visible, layer and frustum, and once for each material group. So an object that is hidden or out of the picture is not counted, a mesh with three materials is three calls, an instanced mesh is one call with every instance in its triangles, and a see-through material with two sides is two calls. Only the first drawing of the scene inside the still is counted: the passes after it (bloom, the lens, the output) draw full-screen quads and are not the scene.
- The check: under the rows stand their sum, what is in no row (`info().counts` less the sum), and `info().counts` itself. Today what is in no row is 1 call of 2 triangles: a full-screen pass of the engine, by its size. The command exits 1 when what is in no row is negative or more than 4 calls, so a row that is missed or counted twice is seen.
- `--live LEVEL` prints the level's limit of draw calls beside the count. The record is `out/work/counts/NAME.json`.
- MEASURED at this commit: `step1-fire` draws 356 calls at `--live low`, `--live medium`, `--live high` and at tier draft. The level changes nothing the renderer draws in a still: `spec.live` only sets the samples to 1 (`src/app.ts`), and `config.live.quality` is read by `src/main.ts` for the pixel ratio and the size of the shadow maps on the site.

## info().textureMb

- `textureMemory` in `src/capture/showcase.ts`. It walks everything that can be reached from the view's scene, the studio's reflection, the light rig and the engine (materials, lights and their shadow maps, render targets with their depth textures), and keeps the textures the renderer has put on the graphics card, by the renderer's own record (`renderer.properties`), each graphics card texture once.
- The bytes of a texture are faces times layers times the texels of its chain times the bytes of a texel. The chain is the levels the texture was given, or the whole chain down to one texel when it makes its own and is read through it, or one level. The bytes of a texel come from the format and the type: 4 for RGBA of bytes, 8 for RGBA half float, 16 for RGBA float. A compressed texture counts the bytes it was given.
- The renderer gives some materials textures of its own: the target it draws through for a material that lets light through (the size of the picture, half float, a whole chain) and a small table for shiny materials (16 by 16). They are in the renderer's record of each material (`renderer.properties.get(material).uniforms`), and the walk reads that record for every material it meets. MEASURED on step 1 with every first version merged, by counting the textures the browser was asked to make (a probe that wraps `createTexture`, `texStorage2D` and `texImage2D`): 34 made and alive beside the renderer's 4 empty ones, 34 in `renderer.info.memory.textures`, 34 read, sizes and kinds the same. Before the walk read that record 32 of 34 were read.
- `renderer.info.memory.textures` is the renderer's own count. What it holds beyond what the walk reached is counted as a target of the size of the picture, RGBA half float with a whole chain, so the number can be too large and not too small. `unseenMb` is 0 on step 1 today.
- A page drawn earlier in the session still holds its textures though the scene no longer leads to them, so the count keeps every texture it has met for as long as the renderer holds it. `info().textureMb[page]` is therefore what the renderer holds after that page was drawn, earlier pages included, as on a visit that goes through the pages in order. MEASURED, one session through all six pages at hd, set `web`: 254.0 MB after the first page, 307.4 after step 1 and after every page that follows, every texture read (31 of 31).
- `info().textureMb[page]` is the number after the last picture drawn of that page in this session, 0 for a page not drawn yet. `info().textures[page]` holds how it is made up: `mb`, `counted`, `held`, `assetMb` (pictures), `targetMb` (what the renderer draws into: shadow maps, the reflection, the engine's own), `unseenMb`, and `largest`, the 12 largest with their name, size and megabytes, for the render budget table. A megabyte is 1048576 bytes.
- It is the memory of textures, not of the graphics card: render buffers with several samples a pixel, vertex buffers and what the driver keeps for itself are not in it.
- MEASURED on step 1 with the stand-ins, at 729ee56: 264.2 MB for the set `web` at hd and 564.8 MB for `full` at uhd, none of it a picture: at uhd the two means of the engine are 126.6 MB each and its sample 63.3, the key light's shadow map 128 (4096 square, colour and depth), the dome's 32, the reflection 4, the renderer's own target 84.4.
- G15 draws the picture at rest of the set `full` through the film's path, so that everything the film's render holds is held when the number is read.

## stillState(): the contrast of type

- `typeContrasts` in `src/capture/showcase.ts`. `stillState` puts the frame into the view and reads the scene the picture would be drawn from. No picture is drawn and no browser is needed.
- The colour of a run is the colour of its mesh: the mesh that carries `userData.type` with the run's key, and `userData.shape` with the run's id when a maker made it. The colour is the material's colour, times its colour picture at that place when the picture is made of numbers that can be read, plus what it gives off.
- The ground is read at five places along the middle line of the run's mesh (0.1 to 0.9 of its length), looking down the axis of the run's own shape, so type on a tilted or turned surface is read against that surface. Type, the cursor and what is hidden are passed through. A surface that lets light through or is faded is mixed with what lies under it, up to six layers. A place over nothing at all is left out: that run is off the page. The middle value of the places counts.
- Both colours are in linear light, weighed 0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722, and the contrast is (lighter + 0.05) / (darker + 0.05), as `kit/07-QUALITY-BAR.md` says for M4.
- Each run of `stillState().type` keeps `contrast` and adds `ink`, `ground` and `contrastFrom`. `contrastFrom` is `materials`, or `stand-in tones` when the scene holds no mesh for the run (a typed field that is empty, or a legend that another shape's object draws without `userData.type`): then the old rule of tones is used. A maker that draws its own legends puts `userData.type` on their meshes, or M4 reads tones for them.
- What it is not: the colour in the finished picture. Light, shadow, the tone curve and the grade are not in it. It is the base colour of the type against the base colour of its ground.
- MEASURED on every key today: every run is read from materials except 2 of the 63 runs of `step1-fire`. The least contrast on step 1 is 5.22, as it was with the tones. On the pinball page `copy:step3.table.modes.1.insert` reads 1.22 (ink 0.040 on 0.060), where the tone rule gave 9.44 before the change: the run lies on the dark floor of a well, and the tone rule skipped wells.
