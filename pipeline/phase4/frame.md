# Frame: the body of the machine

Owner: frame. Parts: `table-frame`, `marquee`, `loader`. Code: `src/table/real/frame/`. Branch: `agent/frame`.

## What a quick look at the approved images misses

1. The outer rail is not one grey bar. It is a bright brushed extrusion with a dark hair of a gap between it and the border of the page, and a lower ledge on the side of the lane.
2. The rails show the row of lamps as a row of glints along their length. The metal is read by what it reflects, not by its own colour.
3. The housing of the marquee is a stepped double bezel: a bright outer frame, a dark inner step, and the display sits in a dark seat of its own under the title.
4. The apron is a slab with a soft bevel standing a little above the page, and the plate at the drain is a small ivory enamel plate with a warm brass edge.
5. The collars of the clear tube are thin bright turned rings with a groove, and the tube is seen mostly by its edges and by what shines in it, not by a tint.

## The clear tube (one spec for the feed, drain and lift tubes)

Written by frame, who started first. All numbers are page pixels (96 px is one ball, 27 mm). They are the `TUBE` object in `src/table/real/frame/loader.ts`.

- Outer radius 59.5 (the stand-in's), wall 4.5, so the bore is 55 and the ball clears it by 7 all round.
- Two shells, the outer facing out and the inner facing in, 24 sides. Front faces only.
- Material: `clearOf(zone)` in `src/table/real/frame/mats.ts`. A physical material with no transmission, colour 0xdfe8ea, roughness 0.06, clearcoat 1, opacity 0.05, depth write off, render order 3. A shader line added after `studio()` and before `lit()` raises the alpha where the wall turns away from the eye (power 1.6, 0.62) and where light shines in it (0.45 of the mean of the outgoing light). Just inside the edge (where one less the cosine to the eye is between 0.6 and 0.96) the wall is darkened by up to 0.72 and its alpha raised by 0.22: the dark line a clear wall shows where it bends the dark of the room to the eye.
- Collars: turned aluminium, 22 long, outer radius 66, a groove 4.8 wide and 1.6 deep in the middle, a 2 px chamfer at each end, two screws on top 0.5 rad either side of the crown. They stand every 3.4 balls measured along the whole tube from its start, so a tube cut at a zone edge has them where one piece would.
- Saddle clamps: a strap 9 wide and 2.4 thick over the top half of the tube, legs down to the surface, a small foot with one screw each side. They stand half a step from each collar, only where the tube lies level and near a surface.
- The tube is cut at each zone edge and built in each zone with that zone's own material.

## Decisions

- The lit zones give metal only the light of the lamps and a dimmed reflection of the studio, so full metal reads near black from above. The frame's aluminium is the page's brushed aluminium (same grain picture, same anisotropy) with metalness 0.4 and roughness 0.4 and a lighter base, so it takes the lamps' light as the approved rails do. The grain picture is the shell's, read 1.6 times finer along the brushing and 3.2 times finer across it, because a rail is seen from closer than the page's own metal. MEASURED in a still: the page's ceramic border beside a rail (no metal, colour 0xecebe6) comes out the same mid grey as the rail's top, so how bright the rails are from here on is the light at page level, not the metal. Colour rides on the corners, so brass, dark and bright aluminium are one drawing.
- A tier is four drawings: metal, paint (border ceramic, dark gaps, bezel, laminate, kerb, enamel), wear strips of bright steel, and one instanced mesh of screws. Budget is eight.
- The border of the page (0.15 balls) is continued down both sides of each well as ceramic with a 1.2 px dark gap to the rail, because the well spans the whole width of the page.
- The left rail: 3 px chamfers, a channel 34 wide and 3 deep along its top with a screw every 1.5 balls, a ledge 18 wide and 7 lower on the lane side, one joint with a 1.4 px gap at 5.6 balls. A fine groove (4.5 wide, 2 deep, 30 below the top) runs along the lane side of the left and right rails.
- The right rail is a cradle. The feed tube's middle is half a ball above the page, so its lowest 11.5 px lie in a round bed of radius 62.5 cut along the rail's top. Lands of 27 px either side carry the screws.
- On the street tier the feed tube dives to the plunger over the last 1.65 balls. There the right rail is an outer wall and a dark pocket.
- The lane walls (60 tall) are built in the lengths `table.segs` gives, so every opening of the layout is an opening of the wall. On the top tier the launch wall is open from the top wall to 0.62 balls below where the `launch` rail crosses it: that is the throat. A corner block cut to a circle of radius 2 balls (centre 0.3 balls left of the playfield's right wall, 2 balls under the top wall) turns the launch lane into the top lane. It clears the ball's path by 0.69 balls or more.
- The right top corner of tiers 2 and 1 has a fillet of radius 0.5 balls, the largest that a ball in the square corner of the physics never enters.
- The cross rail between two tiers is two halves that meet at the zone edge: 0.12 balls of the tier above (56 tall) and 0.3 balls of the tier below (to the same top). The chute's throat is cut through the lower half 0.75 balls either side of the middle, the mouth through the upper half 1.2 balls either side. The step of the floor under it is a dark aluminium riser.
- Above the ball's height (96) the cross rail steps back 11 px to a ledge, and a groove 4.5 wide runs along its face at 64. Its faces toward the tier below are the darker tone, its top and ledge the lighter, so the lamps of the tier fall on it in bands and not as one bright sheet. The approved images show the wall between two tiers as a stepped rail.
- The foot of the street tier is a wall up to the page with a tunnel 122 px tall for the drain.
- The shoulders are not solid blocks. The approved images show floor there with models on it, and the layout stands houses and the display on the floor inside them. Each shoulder is a rim of wall along the layout's segments (`shoulder.step`, `shoulder.edge`, `mouth.side`, `outlane.side`) with turned posts where the lengths meet. The right one holds a dark seat 6 px wide and 35 tall round the display's box. The left one carries a small bright plate with enamel pips: three on the top tier, two in the middle, one on the street.
- Tier trim: a 2.4 px line of brass let into the top of the lane walls, the top wall and the shoulder edges (top tier); a strip of grey laminate 10.4 wide on the lane walls (middle tier); a painted kerb 7 wide and 9 tall at the foot of the lane walls, black and yellow between 5.3 and 6.9 balls down the tier (street). No road sign's place is given to the frame, so the black and yellow stands beside the lane guides. If the owner of the road signs puts one elsewhere, these two numbers move.
- Marquee: everything is built flat in the board's own frame (across, up the board, out of the face) and turned once by `BOARD.turn`. The face is at 0, where the title is set. As in the approved image it is a wide bright frame round two windows: the picture above, a dark display strip the whole width below, an aluminium bar 12 wide between them and a sill with a row of slots under the strip. Outer frame 30 wide and 16 proud with a fine dark line a third of the way in, inner dark step 7 wide and 6 proud round each window, housing 46 deep, 7 screws along the top and the foot of the frame and 4 down each side, a hinge of 7 knuckles under the foot, 12 pairs of vents in the back. The foot is a 9 px plate with 18 bolts; four dark braces and a dark beam under the hinge carry the housing. The lamp lenses of the first version are gone: the approved marquee has none.
- The display strip is centred on the words `marquee-score`, read from `ctx.byId` whether they lie on the page or stand on the board, so its place is the layout's. `POCKET` (472 by 116, the size the owner of the displays asked for, bezel 7 wide and 6 proud, floor 0.9) and `seatOf(zone, ctx)` are exported for that owner.
- The face is one generated picture, `table/frame/marquee-skyline.webp` (recipe in `assets-src/table/frame/RECIPE.md`, take 1 of 1, no letter in it). It is the colour and its own glow. Until it is loaded, and where an asset set lacks it, the face is the skyline drawn by rule in `skyline.ts`; the part's version changes when the picture arrives, so the marquee is built again with it.
- Apron: the two slabs are the ceramic's colour in the frame's own paint with a fine grain (0.03), 16 tall with a 3.2 bevel and 10 px corners, and a brass line 1.5 wide is let into the face of each, 9 px in from its edge, so no slab is a bare face. They were the page's own ceramic material; MEASURED in `table-wide` (sharp average of a 200 by 40 patch) both materials draw the same there, 92,91,88, against 159,155,142 in the approved image, so the tone is the light's and not the material's (see the last section). The card is paper under a clear cover 12 px larger all round on four turned spacers with four screws. The drain plate is ivory enamel inside a brass edge with no screw in its face, as in the approved image, and below it a dark aluminium block with one screw stands either side of the drain mouth. Its words are the layout's `drain-label`; that label was set in the light ink, which does not read on ivory, so `pipeline/phase4/frame-NEEDS.patch` asks for the dark ink (one word in `src/pages/step3/index.ts`).
- Loader: the port and the outlet are a turned ring with a chamfered outer edge, a groove with eight screws, a chamfered lip and a cup with a dark throat. Both are the darker aluminium, as the approved loader page shows a graphite ring. At the port the tube does not start at the middle of the cup, as the `feed` rail does: its mouth stands 6 px inside the wall of the cup, so the ball is seen lying in the cup before it goes, as in the approved image. The tube leaves through a slot cut in the ring (2 px wider than the tube each side, both walls parallel) and lies there on a dark bed. The screws that would fall in the slot are left out. The ball's path is not changed.
- The clear plastic's own colour is a mid grey (0x8a9296), so that it takes little light as a surface and is seen by its edges and its shine, not as milk over what lies behind it.
- The bottoms of the grooves (side rails, top wall, cross rails, the marquee's beam) are the dark gap tone: nothing in the build shades the inside of a groove, so without it a groove does not read.
- The marquee's picture sits inside a second, lower frame of bright aluminium (7 wide, 6 proud), as the approved image shows an inner frame round the picture. The display strip keeps its dark bezel.
- The beam the marquee is hinged on is a ribbed aluminium extrusion (16 thick, a groove every 15 px on both faces), in the darker tone. It was a plain dark block, and from the foot of the table it read as a blank black wall under the marquee.
- The plaque of the left shoulder is placed from the layout's `mouth.side.left` (0.55 balls in from it, 0.21 above its foot), not from typed places.

## For the owner of the lights

- The marquee's face is the mesh `marquee.face.marquee` with `userData.lightGroup = 'marquee.panel'` and `material.userData.lit = true`. Its emissive level is `0.05 + 0.95 * level('panel')`. Nothing else of the marquee gives light.
- The lane lamps stand in the floor of the lanes, so the rails carry no lens recess.

## Found, not the frame's to mend

- The `launch` rail climbs in a straight line through each tier, so a ball in the launch lane lies under the floor plate of the middle and top tiers for most of its way.
- The lift rails do the same in the lift lane.
- The apron and the loader are not among the lit zones (`LIT` in `lights/groups.ts` is marquee, corporate, office, street), so the apron takes only the page's own light. In `table-wide` its near white slabs draw at 92 where the approved image has them at 159.
