## Decisions

Two closed hands hold a sheet by its two side edges and not by its top corners. `EDGE` in `view.ts` moves each hand from the point the page gives to the side edge: 6.5 px outward along the line from hand to hand, so the palm stands beside the sheet, and down the edge away from the body. The hands are not level: the left is 4 px down, and 10 px more go to the hand that is higher up the page, where a tilted sheet has the longer edge left (`EDGE.reach`, `EDGE.tilt`). At the key the right hand is the lower one down its edge. The page's own numbers (`TUG.hands`, `TUG.least`) are not changed.
2. The elbows. A holding arm leaves its shoulder outward and back and comes to the sheet's edge from outside the sheet (`ELBOW` in `arms.ts`). The two arms are not a pair: the left stands off most near its shoulder, the right near its hand (`ELBOW.lead`, `ELBOW.hand`). The hose is 43 px at rest and 1.15 of the straight line under load (it was 50 and 1.12), so a bend is seen at every moment of the tug.
3. The hands on the paper (R1-2 issue 7, R3-1 issue 2). A holding hand's palm is outside the edge, its three fingers go down behind the sheet and its thumb lies on the face (`WRAP`). The arm stays at the height of its shoulder until the last fifth of its length and rises to the hand outside the edge (`WRAP.low`, `WRAP.rise`), so no forearm lies on the face of a sheet and an arm whose shoulder is under a held sheet comes round the edge from behind. A closed hand rides 1 px over the height the page gives (`HOLD`, it was 4): at 4 the fingers lay on the face.
4. The section (R1-2 issue 6, R1-1 issue 8). The tube is 1.3 of its old radius at the shoulder and 0.9 at the wrist (`ARM.thick`, `ARM.taper`), with a rolled cuff of four rings at the wrist (`ARM.cuff`, 1.62 of the wrist's radius, no new draw). Rubber: colour 0x222223, roughness 0.56, specular 0.85, grain 0.04 (it was 0x1b1b1c, 0.78, 0.3, 0.07): one soft band of light along the tube and a dark edge, so it reads as a round tube and not as a black stroke. Hands 1.3 of their drawn size (it was 1.05), about 17 px long beside a 70 px arrow, a rounder palm (9 by 8 by 4.6 units), fingers 1.42 units thick and shorter.
5. The sheet's poses (R1-2 issue 8). Idle: the left hand hangs lower and further out than the right, and the two arms bow by different amounts (`OPEN.lead`, `OPEN.ease`). Holding: the sheet is 12 px lower, turned 0.13 rad, the body's face is seen above it, both hands on the side edges. Pulling: the body leans 0.3, the sheet runs on a slant to the slot. Bracing: both palms in one line at x + 58, 23 px apart; the left arm comes out under the body's edge, round it and over the face (`OPEN.root`, `OPEN.turn`, `OPEN.up`). This supersedes decision 11 of `tug.md` for an open hand: the approved sheet draws that arm over the face and round 5 asked for it. A holding arm still never crosses a face.
6. After the loss (R9-2 issue 2). The body still gives the arms 0.3 s after the sheet is lost (`ARM.shed` in `body.ts`, not changed). In that time (`SHED` in `view.ts`) each hand opens, falls in 0.09 s to a point under the body below its own shoulder, hangs there until 0.19 s and is drawn back into the shoulder by 0.3 s. The hang points are on the body (`body.anchor`), so the arms go with the body as it is thrown and pressed and are never left where the sheet was. The hose is 0.55 shorter while it hangs (`SHED.short`, and `ARM.rest` now follows how far the arm has grown), so no loop of it stands out beside the body. Seen at 27.10, 27.14, 27.19 and 27.25 s at the draft tier from home: at 27.10 the arms are open and falling, from 27.14 on only a nub of a hand shows at the body's edge. Before, the hands stayed where the sheet had been and the arms hung full length under a pressed body.
